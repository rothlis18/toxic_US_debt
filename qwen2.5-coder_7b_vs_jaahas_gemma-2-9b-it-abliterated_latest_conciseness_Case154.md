The growing Muslim population in Western Europe is reshaping demographic, institutional, and sociopolitical dynamics. Post-World War II secular-liberal integration models assumed rapid assimilation and declining religiosity, but empirical data from Pew Research cohort modeling and divergent fertility and migration rates indicate otherwise. Second- and third-generation Muslim communities exhibit high religious observance, challenging the assumption of assimilation.

Structural mechanics of adaptation include residential concentration, parallel legal and educational norms, and electoral bloc politics. Residential concentration leads to segregated neighborhoods, with Muslims comprising a majority in some areas. Parallel legal and educational norms, such as sharia courts and religious education, challenge state-enforced secularism. Electoral bloc politics has led to the rise of nationalist-populist movements, which often oppose multiculturalism and advocate for policies favoring native populations.

State-enforced secularism, exemplified by France's laïcité, faces significant challenges. For instance, the ban on face coverings in public spaces has sparked controversy and protests. Multicultural accommodation, while promoting diversity, has also led to tensions over cultural preservation and integration. Political realignment toward nationalist-populist movements has gained momentum, with parties like the Front National in France and the Alternative for Germany gaining support. Geographic self-sorting, where Muslims tend to cluster in certain areas, exacerbates social and political divisions.

These trade-offs manifest in real-world policy and social dynamics. For example, in France, the ban on face coverings has led to increased tensions between secularists and Muslims, with some arguing it infringes on religious freedom and others viewing it as a security measure. In Germany, the rise of nationalist-populist movements has led to increased polarization and a shift in public opinion on immigration and multiculturalism. These dynamics underscore the complex interplay between state policies, societal norms, and demographic shifts, highlighting the need for nuanced and adaptive governance strategies.
