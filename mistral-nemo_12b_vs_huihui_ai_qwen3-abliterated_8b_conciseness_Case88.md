Muslim population growth in Western Europe, driven by higher fertility rates (3.5 vs. 1.5 for non-Muslims) and migration, challenges post-WWII secular-liberal integration models. Demographic shifts drive institutional adaptations: by 2050, Muslims may comprise 14% of EU population, reshaping education (parallel Islamic schools) and law (sharia-compliant finance, halal food regulations). Residential concentration and electoral blocs reshape governance, with Muslims voting more conservatively, impacting municipal policies and welfare-state distribution. State-enforced secularism (e.g., French laïcité) risks alienating Muslims, multicultural accommodation may foster parallel societies, while nationalist-populist realignment risks social unrest. Systems-level feedback loops risk destabilizing the welfare state, with Muslim populations potentially straining resources while contributing less due to lower employment rates (55% vs. 70% for non-Muslims).
