The growing Muslim population in Western Europe, projected to reach 30% of the total population by 2050 according to Pew Research, challenges the post-World War II secular-liberal integration model, which assumed rapid assimilation and declining religiosity. Muslim fertility rates, at 2.1 children per woman, are significantly higher than the 1.5 for native European populations, driving population growth and residential concentration. Parallel legal and educational norms, such as separate religious schools and family courts, exacerbate social fragmentation. Electoral bloc politics, with Muslim voters increasingly aligning with nationalist-populist parties, reshapes municipal governance. For instance, in France, the ban on face veils (laïcité) has led to increased social tensions and legal challenges, while in Germany, multicultural policies have resulted in higher welfare dependency rates among Muslim communities. Quantitative models show that state-enforced secularism, like France's laïcité, reduces religious observance but may stifle cultural expression and social cohesion. Conversely, multicultural accommodation policies, such as Germany's, increase social integration but strain welfare systems. Political realignment toward nationalist-populist movements, exemplified by the rise of parties like the National Front in France and the Alternative for Germany (AfD), further polarizes societies and restricts free speech. Geographic self-sorting, where Muslims cluster in urban areas, intensifies social and economic disparities. The trade-offs between these strategies include economic costs, social cohesion, and political stability. Quantitative analysis reveals that multicultural policies, while costly, yield higher social integration and lower crime rates compared to secularist approaches. However, they also lead to higher welfare dependency and increased polarization. Nationalist-populist policies, while reducing welfare costs, significantly decrease social cohesion and freedom of expression. Therefore, a balanced approach that combines elements of multiculturalism with limited state-enforced secularism, while addressing economic disparities and promoting civic engagement, offers the most sustainable solution for long-term integration and social stability.
