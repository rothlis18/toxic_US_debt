**Structural Consequences:**

1. **Debt-Service Costs:** The U.S. Treasury would face an insurmountable challenge in servicing its existing debt. Without new issuance, the Treasury would rely on existing cash reserves and future tax revenues. However, the compounding debt-service costs on the existing debt would accelerate, making it increasingly difficult to service the debt without default.

2. **Yield Spikes:** As the market recognizes the increased risk of default, yields on Treasury bonds would spike. This would lead to a catastrophic increase in interest rates, which would have a ripple effect across the entire economy, causing borrowing costs to skyrocket for consumers and businesses.

3. **Banking Insolvency:** Many domestic banks and financial institutions have significant exposures to Treasury bonds. If these institutions cannot roll over their debt or if the value of their Treasury holdings plummets, they would face insolvency, triggering a broader financial crisis.

4. **Pension Collapse:** Many pension funds have invested heavily in Treasury bonds, expecting them to be a safe, long-term investment. With yields spiking, these funds would face severe deficits, making it impossible for them to meet their liabilities, leading to widespread pension collapses.

5. **Failed Treasury Auctions:** Without demand, Treasury auctions would fail, further exacerbating the crisis. The Treasury would be unable to rollover its debt, leading to a liquidity crisis.

**Monetary Consequences:**

1. **Federal Reserve's Dilemma:** The Federal Reserve would be forced to choose between two undesirable options: allow catastrophic yield spikes, leading to domestic banking insolvency and pension collapse, or engage in overt yield-curve control and debt monetization. The latter would accelerate currency debasement and potentially trigger stagflationary feedback loops.

2. **Inflation:** The Fed might attempt to combat the crisis through inflation, but this would further erode the purchasing power of the dollar, exacerbating the crisis.

**Geopolitical Consequences:**

1. **Loss of Reserve Currency Status:** The dollar would likely lose its status as the world's primary reserve currency. This would have significant implications for global trade and finance, as countries would need to diversify their reserves away from the dollar.

2. **Global Financial Unwind:** The loss of the 'risk-free' Treasury benchmark would trigger collateral liquidations across repo markets. This would force countries to settle trade outside the SWIFT/dollar system, leading to a fragmentation of the global financial system.

3. **Bilateral Trade:** Without the dollar as a common medium, countries would need to engage in bilateral trade agreements, potentially leading to a more fragmented and less efficient global economy.

4. **Domestic Austerity:** The U.S. government would be forced to implement severe austerity measures to avoid default, including cuts to entitlement programs and public services.

1. **Debt-to-GDP Ratio:** The U.S. debt-to-GDP ratio would skyrocket, potentially exceeding 150% or more, as the Treasury struggles to service its existing debt.

2. **Interest Rates:** Interest rates could spike to 10% or more, leading to a severe recession.

4. **Inflation:** Inflation could exceed 10%, eroding the purchasing power of the dollar and exacerbating the crisis.
