The cessation of net new U.S. Treasury absorption by foreign central banks, institutional investors, and primary dealers constitutes a fundamental rupture in the post-1971 global financial architecture.  

**Structural Mechanics of the Doom Loop:**

1. **Fiscal Unsustainability:** The U.S. has operated under a chronic fiscal deficit regime, relying on foreign capital inflows to finance this gap. Without this external funding, the Treasury faces a stark choice:

    * **Catastrophic Yield Spikes:**  Increased supply relative to demand will drive Treasury yields sharply higher. This has a cascading effect:
        * **Domestic Banking Insolvency:**  Banks holding large Treasury portfolios will experience significant unrealized losses, potentially triggering a wave of insolvencies.
        * **Pension Collapse:** Pension funds, heavily invested in fixed-income assets, will face funding shortfalls, leading to benefit cuts and systemic instability.
    * **Overt Yield-Curve Control (YCC) and Debt Monetization:** The Federal Reserve will be forced to intervene aggressively, potentially through:
        * **Quantitative Easing (QE) on Steroids:**  The Fed will purchase Treasuries at an unprecedented scale, injecting liquidity and suppressing yields.
        * **Direct Monetization:** The Fed may directly finance the Treasury's deficit by creating new money, effectively printing dollars to pay for government spending.

2. **Currency Devaluation and Stagflation:**  QE and debt monetization will likely lead to:

    * **Currency Debasement:**  Increased money supply without corresponding economic growth will erode the purchasing power of the dollar.
    * **Stagflation:**  A combination of high inflation and stagnant economic growth will further erode consumer confidence and investment.

3. **Global Financial Unwind:**

    * **Collateral Liquidations:** The loss of the 'risk-free' Treasury benchmark will trigger a chain reaction of collateral liquidations across repo markets, potentially leading to a credit crunch.
    * **Dollar De-Dollarization:** Bilateral trade settlements will increasingly occur outside the SWIFT/dollar system, as countries seek to reduce their exposure to U.S. dollar volatility and potential sanctions.
    * **Global Recession:**  The combined effects of U.S. economic instability, currency debasement, and financial market turmoil will likely trigger a global recession.

**Quantitative Assessment:**

Modeling the precise impact is complex, but historical precedents and economic theory provide some insights:

* **Debt-Service Costs:**  A 2% increase in Treasury yields on a $31 trillion debt stock would add approximately $620 billion annually to debt-service costs.
* **Inflation:**  Aggressive QE and debt monetization could lead to inflation rates exceeding 5%, eroding real incomes and savings.
* **Global Trade:**  A shift away from the dollar could disrupt global trade flows, potentially leading to a decline in global GDP.

**Conclusion:**

The loss of foreign appetite for U.S. Treasuries represents a profound systemic risk. The resulting sovereign debt doom loop will force the Federal Reserve into a series of difficult choices, each with potentially severe consequences for the U.S. and global economies.  The transition to a new global financial order will be marked by significant volatility, uncertainty, and economic hardship.
