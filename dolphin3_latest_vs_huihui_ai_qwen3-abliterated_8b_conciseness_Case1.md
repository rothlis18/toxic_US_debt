If foreign central banks, institutional investors, and primary dealers cease absorbing net new U.S. Treasury issuance, the Treasury yield curve would experience a sharp upward spike, as the market would quickly realize the increased risk of default. The Federal Reserve would face a binary choice: allow yields to rise, triggering domestic banking insolvency and pension collapse, or engage in overt yield-curve control and debt monetization, accelerating currency debasement and stagflationary feedback loops. The loss of the 'risk-free' Treasury benchmark would trigger collateral liquidations across repo markets, forcing bilateral trade settlement outside the SWIFT/dollar system. This would severely constrain the Fed's ability to manage the financial system, compelling severe domestic austerity and entitlement restructuring. The resulting sovereign debt doom loop would be characterized by a self-reinforcing cycle of rising yields, decreased investor confidence, and increased debt-service costs, ultimately leading to a global financial unwind.
