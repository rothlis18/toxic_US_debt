By 2050, Europe's Muslim population, projected to reach 17% (Pew), will challenge secular-liberal integration models due to divergent fertility rates (3.5 vs. 1.5), persistent religiosity, and migration. Residential concentration in urban areas (e.g., 40% of Muslims in France live in Seine-Saint-Denis) fosters parallel legal and educational norms, reshaping governance (e.g., halal food provision in schools). Electoral bloc politics shift power dynamics, with Muslims voting overwhelmingly left (63% in France) and influencing policies. Non-Muslim Europeans adapt via state-enforced secularism (e.g., France's 2004 headscarf ban), multicultural accommodation (e.g., UK's faith schools), nationalist-populist realignment (e.g., Le Pen's 33% in 2017), and geographic self-sorting (e.g., non-Muslims leaving high-concentration areas). Trade-offs include social cohesion vs. cultural preservation, fiscal burden vs. economic growth, and security vs. civil liberties.
